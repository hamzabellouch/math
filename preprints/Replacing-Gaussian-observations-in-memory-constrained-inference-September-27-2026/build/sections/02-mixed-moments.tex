\section{A projection moment with shared and fresh rows}
\label{sec:r-mixed-moments}

The information bound in
Proposition~\ref{prop:r-information-comparison} contains a projection
density evaluated at the true label.  The rows used to select the
message can be biased after conditioning on that message.  We first
bound a high moment under the original Gaussian row law.  Some rows
are shared across all factors in this moment; the other rows are
averaged independently in each factor.  The high moment will then
pay for the change from Gaussian rows to selected rows.

Expanding a projection moment into independent signal copies is part
of the projection method of Sharan, Sidford, and Valiant
\cite[Section~7, Lemma~12]{SSV2019}.  The estimate below treats exact
multirow projections.  Its geometric hypothesis has two local mass
bounds: one controls distance to a span, and the other controls the
small radial scales.

For \(x>0\), write \(\log^+x=\max\{\log x,0\}\).

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Mixed projection moment and exact density version]
\label{thm:r-mixed-moment}
Let \(n=d-1\), let \(m,r,q\ge1\) be integers, and put
\(\ell=m+r\).  Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-dimensions}
 \ell<n,\qquad \ell-m-(q-1)\ge1.
\end{equation}
Let \(\rho\) be a finite nonnegative Borel measure on \(S^{d-1}\).
Suppose that \(B,D>0\) and, for every \(z\in\R^d\) and \(t>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-growth}
 \rho(B(z,t))\le Bt^\ell,\qquad
 \rho(B(z,t))\le Dt^n.
\end{equation}
Let \(X\sim\gamma_m\) and \(Z\sim\gamma_r\) be independent, and set
\(G=(X;Z)\).  For \(\delta>0\), define the cube average
\[
 p_{\rho,\delta}(g,y)
   =(2\delta)^{-\ell}\int
       \ind_{\{\norm{gu-y}_\infty\le\delta\}}\,d\rho(u).
\]
There is an absolute \(C\) such that, for every fixed
\(s\in S^{d-1}\) and every \(\delta>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-smoothed}
 \left[\E_X
       \left(\E_Z p_{\rho,\delta}((X;Z),(X;Z)s)\right)^q
 \right]^{1/q}
 \le e^{Cd}B\bigl(1+\log^+(D/B)\bigr).
\end{equation}

If in addition \(\rho\ll\sigma\), use the jointly Borel density
\(p_\rho\) in \eqref{eq:r-density-version}.  There is a measurable
set \(E_\rho\subset S^{d-1}\), with \(\rho(E_\rho)=0\), such that
for every \(s\notin E_\rho\) the cube averages converge to
\(p_\rho(G,Gs)\) for \(\gamma_\ell\)-almost every \(G\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-exact}
 \left[\E_X
       \left(\E_Zp_\rho((X;Z),(X;Z)s)\right)^q
 \right]^{1/q}
 \le e^{Cd}B\bigl(1+\log^+(D/B)\bigr).
\end{equation}
When \(\rho\ne0\), \(E_\rho\) may also be chosen so that
\(0<p_\rho(G,Gs)<\infty\) for \(\gamma_\ell\)-almost every \(G\)
and every \(s\notin E_\rho\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We begin with the inverse-distance integral that appears after
expanding the moment.  Fix \(s\in S^{d-1}\) and a linear subspace
\(V\subset\R^d\) of dimension \(h\le q-1\).  On a radial shell
\[
 R/2<\norm{u-s}\le R,\qquad R=2^{1-i},\quad i\ge0,
\]
consider the points satisfying
\(\dist(u-s,V)\le\eta R\), where \(0<\eta\le1\).  Project them
onto the radius-\(R\) ball in \(s+V\).  An \(\eta R\)-net of this
ball has at most \((3/\eta)^h\) points: a maximal separated set
has disjoint \(h\)-balls of radius \(\eta R/2\), all lying in the
ball of radius \(R+\eta R/2\).  For \(h=0\), take the single
point of the zero-dimensional ball.  Every point under consideration
is within \(2\eta R\) of one of the net points.  The ambient-center
bounds in \eqref{eq:r-mixed-growth} therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-tube}
 \rho\{R/2<\norm{u-s}\le R,\ \dist(u-s,V)\le\eta R\}
 \le e^{Cd}\min\{BR^\ell\eta^{\ell-h},
                         DR^n\eta^{n-h}\}.
\end{equation}
Both powers of \(\eta\) are positive, so the part of the shell at
zero distance from \(V\) has zero \(\rho\)-mass.  The ball bounds
also give \(\rho(\{s\})=0\), by sending their radius to zero.

On the band
\(2^{-a-1}R<\dist(u-s,V)\le2^{-a}R\), \(a\ge0\), we have
\[
 \norm{u-s}^{-r}\dist(u-s,V)^{-m}
 \le 2^{r+m}R^{-\ell}2^{am}.
\]
Summing \eqref{eq:r-mixed-tube} over these bands with either of its
two terms gives a geometric series.  The relevant exponents satisfy
\[
 \ell-h-m\ge\ell-(q-1)-m\ge1,\qquad
 n-h-m\ge1.
\]
Thus the integral on one radial shell is at most
\[
 e^{Cd}\min\{B,DR^{\,n-\ell}\}.
\]
The shells \(R=2^{1-i}\) cover all nonzero distances on the unit
sphere.  Put \(b=n-\ell\ge1\).  Their sum obeys
\[
 \sum_{i\ge0}\min\{B,D2^{b(1-i)}\}
 \le C B\bigl(1+\log^+(D/B)\bigr).
\]
For example, after the \(i=0\) term, if \(D/B\le1\) the terms form
a geometric tail of ratio at most \(1/2\).  If \(D/B>1\), at most
\(1+\log_2(D/B)/b\) further terms equal \(B\), followed by such a
tail.  We have proved the uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-inverse}
 \int\norm{u-s}^{-r}\dist(u-s,V)^{-m}\,d\rho(u)
 \le e^{Cd}B\bigl(1+\log^+(D/B)\bigr).
\end{equation}

We now expand the projection moment.  For fixed \(u\ne s\), the
\(r\) coordinates of \(Z(u-s)\) are independent centered normals
of variance \(\norm{u-s}^2\).  Bounding each cube probability by
its volume times the peak density shows that the fresh-row average,
after division by \((2\delta)^r\), is at most
\((2\pi)^{-r/2}\norm{u-s}^{-r}\).
Expand the \(q\)th power of the remaining integral over
\(u_1,\ldots,u_q\).  Write \(v_i=u_i-s\).  For one shared row of
\(X\), the vector of its \(q\) evaluations on the \(v_i\) is
Gaussian with covariance \(\Gram(v_1,\ldots,v_q)\).  On the set
where this matrix is nonsingular, its peak density bounds its cube
probability by
\[
 (2\delta)^q(2\pi)^{-q/2}
       \det\Gram(v_1,\ldots,v_q)^{-1/2}.
\]
The singular tuples have zero \(\rho^{\otimes q}\)-mass:
successively, \eqref{eq:r-mixed-tube} gives zero mass to
\(s+\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_{i-1})\).  Independence of
the \(m\) shared rows and the \(r\) fresh rows cancels all cube
volumes and bounds the expanded moment by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-mixed-collision}
 (2\pi)^{-\ell q/2}
 \int\prod_{i=1}^q\norm{v_i}^{-r}\,
       \det\Gram(v_1,\ldots,v_q)^{-m/2}\,d\rho^{\otimes q}.
\end{equation}
Gram--Schmidt gives
\[
 \det\Gram(v_1,\ldots,v_q)
   =\prod_{i=1}^q
       \dist\bigl(v_i,\operatorname{span}(v_1,\ldots,v_{i-1})\bigr)^2,
\]
with the empty span equal to \(\{0\}\).  Integrate in the reverse
order \(u_q,\ldots,u_1\).  Every inner integral is bounded by
\eqref{eq:r-mixed-inverse}, uniformly in the previous vectors.
This proves \eqref{eq:r-mixed-smoothed}.  All integrands used in
the expansion are nonnegative, so Tonelli justifies each
interchange.

Suppose now that \(\rho\ll\sigma\).  For every full-rank \(g\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:r-projection-density} and Lebesgue differentiation
give cube convergence outside a Lebesgue-null subset of label
space.  Its inverse image is \(\rho\)-null because \(g_\#\rho\)
has a density.  The same argument shows that the exact density is
finite and, when \(\rho\ne0\), positive at \(gs\) for
\(\rho\)-almost every \(s\).  Since \(\gamma_\ell\) gives full
row rank almost surely, Fubini supplies a single
\(\rho\)-null set \(E_\rho\) with these properties for every
\(s\notin E_\rho\) and almost every independent \(G\).

Fix such an \(s\).  Fatou in the fresh rows gives
\[
 \E_Zp_\rho((X;Z),(X;Z)s)
 \le\liminf_{j\to\infty}
       \E_Zp_{\rho,1/j}((X;Z),(X;Z)s)
\]
for almost every \(X\).  Raise to the \(q\)th power and use Fatou
again in \(X\).  The uniform bound
\eqref{eq:r-mixed-smoothed} then proves
\eqref{eq:r-mixed-exact}.  This last assertion has the stated
\(\rho\)-almost-everywhere qualification.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Changing the actual row law before taking the density limit}
\label{subsec:r-actual-row-limit}

The exceptional set in the exact moment theorem is measured using
\(\rho\).  A selected message produces a different conditional law
of the true signal and the actual rows.  The following form uses the
uniform cube estimate for every signal, pays for that row selection,
and then takes the density limit under the actual label law.  The
localization application uses this order in its own specialized proof.

\begin{proposition}[Actual-row tilt and exact-label limit]
\label{prop:r-actual-row-limit}
Keep the dimensions of Theorem~\ref{thm:r-mixed-moment}.  Let
\(\nu\le H\sigma\), \(H<\infty\), be a probability measure.
Let \(S\sim\nu\) and \(X\sim\gamma_m\) be independent, let \(W\)
be countable with \(H(W)<\infty\) and any joint law with
\((S,X)\), and let \(Z\sim\gamma_r\) be independent of
\((S,X,W)\).  Put \(G=(X;Z)\) and \(Y=GS\).
For every positive-probability \(w\), let \(\rho_w\ll\sigma\)
be a finite nonnegative measure satisfying
\eqref{eq:r-mixed-growth} with constants \(B_w,D_w>0\), and set
\[
 C_w=e^{Cd}B_w\bigl(1+\log^+(D_w/B_w)\bigr),
\]
where \(C\) is the constant in the theorem.  Assume
\(\E\log(1+C_W)<\infty\).

The actual conditional law of \(Y\) given \((G,W)\) has a
Lebesgue density.  With the versions
\eqref{eq:r-density-version},
\[
 p_{\rho_W,1/j}(G,Y)\longrightarrow p_{\rho_W}(G,Y)
 \quad\text{almost surely under the actual law}.
\]
For every \(\delta>0\), and also for the exact density,
\begin{align}
 \E\log\bigl(1+p_{\rho_W,\delta}(G,Y)\bigr)
 &\le \frac{I(X;W\mid S)}q+\E\log(1+C_W),
 \label{eq:r-actual-smoothed}\\
 \E\log\bigl(1+p_{\rho_W}(G,Y)\bigr)
 &\le \frac{I(X;W\mid S)}q+\E\log(1+C_W)
 \le \frac{H(W)}q+\E\log(1+C_W).
 \label{eq:r-actual-exact}
\end{align}
In particular \(\log^+p_{\rho_W}(G,Y)\) is integrable, and the
extended expectation of \(\log p_{\rho_W}(G,Y)\), with
\(\log0=-\infty\), is defined and bounded above by the same
right-hand side.  For a deterministic countable refinement
\(W'=(W,E(S,W))\) of finite entropy, the row charge is unchanged:
\[
 I(X;W'\mid S)=I(X;W\mid S).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For almost every \((s,w)\), let \(K_{s,w}\) be the conditional law
of \(X\).  Since \(X\) and \(S\) are initially independent, the
conditional chain rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-conditional-row-kl}
 \E_{S,W}D(K_{S,W}\Vert\gamma_m)
   =I(X;W\mid S)\le H(W)<\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus \(K_{s,w}\ll\gamma_m\) for almost every \((s,w)\).
For fixed \(s,w,\delta\), define
\[
 F_{w,\delta}(s,x)=
   \E_Zp_{\rho_w,\delta}((x;Z),(x;Z)s).
\]
The every-signal estimate \eqref{eq:r-mixed-smoothed} says that
\(\|F_{w,\delta}(s,\cdot)\|_{L^q(\gamma_m)}\le C_w\), uniformly
in \(\delta\).  Conditional on \((S,X,W)\), \(Z\) still has its
independent Gaussian law.  Jensen in \(Z\), followed by
\eqref{eq:r-entropy-variational} with the nonnegative test
\(q\log(1+F_{w,\delta})\), gives
\begin{align*}
 \E\!\left[\log(1+p_{\rho_w,\delta}(G,Y))
             \,\middle|\,S=s,W=w\right]
 &\le \E_{K_{s,w}}\log(1+F_{w,\delta}(s,X))\\
 &\le \frac{D(K_{s,w}\Vert\gamma_m)}q
       +\frac1q\log\E_{\gamma_m}(1+F_{w,\delta}(s,X))^q\\
 &\le \frac{D(K_{s,w}\Vert\gamma_m)}q+\log(1+C_w).
\end{align*}
The last step is the \(L^q\) triangle inequality, valid since
\(q\ge1\).  Average over \((S,W)\) and use
\eqref{eq:r-conditional-row-kl} to obtain
\eqref{eq:r-actual-smoothed}.

It remains to specify the null set for the limit in the actual
experiment.  The marginal law of \((G,S)\) is
\(\gamma_\ell\otimes\nu\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:r-projection-density}
therefore gives a Lebesgue density for the actual conditional law
of \(Y\) given \((G,W)\).  For each \(w\) and every full-rank \(g\),
Lebesgue differentiation for \(g_\#\rho_w\) says that
\(p_{\rho_w,1/j}(g,y)\to p_{\rho_w}(g,y)\) outside a
Lebesgue-null set of \(y\).  The actual conditional density gives
that set zero probability.  Gaussian \(G\) has full rank almost
surely, and there are only countably many \(w\), proving the
asserted almost-sure convergence under the actual law.

The functions \(\log(1+p_{\rho_W,1/j}(G,Y))\) are nonnegative.
Fatou applied now, after the row-law change, passes
\eqref{eq:r-actual-smoothed} to \eqref{eq:r-actual-exact}.
Since \(\log^+x\le\log(1+x)\), the positive logarithmic part is
integrable; the negative part may have infinite mean, in which
case the extended expectation of \(\log p_{\rho_W}\) is
\(-\infty\) and the upper bound remains valid.  Applications
that subtract this logarithm verify its full integrability
separately.  Finally, \(E\) is determined by \((S,W)\), so
\(I(X;E\mid S,W)=0\); the chain rule proves the refinement identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The parameters used by the localization potential]
\label{rem:r-localization-substitution}
The inverse-distance potential in the localization companion
\cite[Section~12, Lemma~12.5]{OpenAILocalization2026} takes
\[
 m=r=q=k=\lfloor d/16\rfloor,\qquad \ell=p=2k,\qquad n=d-1.
\]
For its bounded-density probability \(\nu\le H\sigma\), it
defines \(J_\nu(s)=\int\norm{u-s}^{-p}\,d\nu(u)\) and
\(d\rho=d\nu/J_\nu\).  The application proves locally that
\[
 \rho(B(z,t))\le 2^p t^p,\qquad
 \rho(B(z,t))\le 2^{p+n}H t^n.
\]
Thus the precise constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:r-mixed-moment}
are \(B=2^p\) and \(D=2^{p+n}H\), the angular margin is
\(p-k-(k-1)=1\), and the radial margin is \(n-p>0\).
The theorem supplies the uniform cube estimate and its exact-density
scope.  The localization application imports the positive-scale
estimate and proves its reweighting, specialized row-law change,
and actual-law Fatou passage locally, together with its potential
calculations.
Proposition~\ref{prop:r-actual-row-limit} gives a general comparison
of the same type within the present paper.
\end{remark}
