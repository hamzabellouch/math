\section{The Hamiltonian counterexample}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We now apply the finite-order reduction to the function on the fixed
surface and compare the resulting fixed-point count with the ambient
critical number and cup length.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}]
Take the symplectic quadric $(M,\omega)$ and Hamiltonian involution $A$
from Proposition~\ref{prop:quadric}. Its fixed submanifold $F$ is
diffeomorphic to $S^2\times S^2$.
Transport the function of Proposition~\ref{prop:three-points} to $F$;
it has exactly three critical points.

Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} provides a smooth $A$-invariant extension
$K\colon M\to\R$ with $K|_F=f$.
Let $B_s$ denote its Hamiltonian flow. For all sufficiently small
$\varepsilon>0$, the map
\[
  \phi=A\circ B_\varepsilon
\]
is a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism and satisfies
$\Fix(\phi)=\CritSet(f)$, with the critical points viewed as points of $F$.
The exact comparison in Corollary~\ref{cor:quadric-critical} now gives
\[
  \#\Fix(\phi)=3<4=\Crit(M)
     =\operatorname{cuplength}(M;\mathbb Q).
\]
It remains to verify the asserted degeneracy. Write
$\omega_F=\omega|_F$, which is symplectic,
and let $X_f$ be the Hamiltonian vector field of $f$ on $(F,\omega_F)$.
Invariance of $K$ makes $X_K$ tangent to $F$, and restricting its defining
equation to $TF$ gives $X_K|_F=X_f$. Indeed, at $q\in F$ invariance gives
$dA_qX_K(q)=X_K(q)$, and the fixed tangent space is $T_qF$.
Thus $B_s$ preserves $F$, and its restriction is the Hamiltonian flow
of $f$ on $F$. At the critical point $q_0$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:degenerate-critical-point}, differentiating
$\iota_{X_f}\omega_F=df$ gives
\[
  \omega_F\bigl(DX_f(q_0)v,w\bigr)
  =\operatorname{Hess}_{q_0}f(v,w).
\]
Thus any nonzero vector $v$ in the Hessian kernel satisfies
$DX_f(q_0)v=0$. Since $A|_F=\id$ and $q_0$ is an equilibrium,
\[
  d\phi_{q_0}v
   =\exp\bigl(\varepsilon DX_f(q_0)\bigr)v
   =v.
\]
The fixed point $q_0$ therefore has eigenvalue $1$ in its linearization.
This argument takes place entirely in the invariant subspace $T_{q_0}F$;
no assumption on the normal Hessian of the extension $K$ is needed.
\end{proof}
