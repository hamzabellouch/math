## Local proof interfaces

- **Invariant-kernel obstruction (Section 2).** The block-row and block-column Hilbert–Schmidt calculations give the lower bound on distance from the left-translation commutant. The direct-sum proposition proves the bounded cocycle identity, the triangular representation's group law and all-group norm bound, the invariant-complement implication, and compression to every summand. It assumes no common bound on similarity operators across representations.
- **Sparse assignments (Section 3).** The contracting average is proved from approximate invariant vectors and a limit mean. Indicator-vector pairing gives the rectangular edge-count bound. The finite matching argument and countable finitely branching extension are supplied. The endpoint copies and all edge labels remain distinct, including equal group products.
- **Matrix weights and norm bounds (Section 4).** The normalized-sign construction, scalar exponential tail, finite sphere nets and support union bound are proved with their stated constants. The edge-space analysis/multiplication/synthesis factorization proves the operator bound, including repeated labels. Its three applications give the row, column and uniform conjugation-difference bounds.
- **Assembly (Section 5).** The integer parameters satisfy the displayed dimension ratio limit. Positive rank-one weights give energy at least the number of labels. One fixed Hilbert direct sum then has no bounded cocycle implementer. Applying the direct-sum obstruction to rescaled stage operators gives a nonunitarizable representation with uniform bound at most $1+\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. The finite-test Følner criterion supplies a finitely generated nonamenable subgroup of every nonamenable discrete group. The explicit induced representation has the same uniform bound, and compression of any invariant equivalent inner product to its identity-coset summand would unitarize the subgroup representation. The amenable direction is the Day–Dixmier averaged-inner-product proof, included explicitly.
