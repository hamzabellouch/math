\section{Shared data and distance-bin comparisons}
\label{sec:distance-bins}

The determinant test in the preceding section pays a double logarithm of
the largest prior density. We instead encode the dyadic scale of each
projected simplex height. Under a prior bounded by $e^b$, these scales
have entropy $O(1+b/d)$. The projected correlation recovers the height
and label-density terms, leaving this scale cost. Since the chosen scale
depends on the side matrix, the central step compares it with a mixture
over fixed scales before averaging the matrix.

Throughout this section, for sufficiently large $d$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-dimensions}
 k=\lfloor d/16\rfloor,\qquad q=\lfloor d/8\rfloor,\qquad
 p=\lfloor d/2\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Here $k$ is the number of actual rows, $q$ is the number of replicas,
and $p$ is the number of side rows. In particular $k\ge d/32$,
$q\ge d/16$, $q\ge2$, and $p-i+2\ge2k$ for $2\le i\le q$.

\begin{theorem}[Information from shared exact data]\label{thm:distance-block}
Let $P=f\sigma_d$ be a probability with a nonnegative Borel density
$f\le e^b$, where $b\ge0$. Let $S\sim P$, let $A$ be an independent
$k\times d$ standard Gaussian matrix, and put $Y=AS$. Let a finite message
$W$ be drawn from a kernel of $(A,Y)$, conditionally independently of $S$.
Let $B$ be a $p\times d$ standard Gaussian matrix independent of the
entire joint experiment producing $(S,A,Y,W)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-block}
 I(S;W\mid B,BS)\le\frac{H(W)}q+C\left(d+\frac bd\right)
\end{equation}
for an absolute $C$, uniformly over the prior and message kernel.
\end{theorem}

The order of \eqref{eq:distance-block} also follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-block}: append $k$ ignored fresh rows to $A$
and first use the initial $8k$ rows of $B$. The Gaussian theorem gives
the entropy denominator $2k+1\ge q$, and $8k\le p$. Adding the remaining
independent side rows can only decrease the conditional information,
because independence of the side matrix gives
\[
 I(S;W\mid B,BS)=I(S;W)-I(W;B,BS).
\]
Finally $\log(2+b)\le\log(2+d)+b/d\le Cd+b/d$. The direct proof below
identifies the entropy of the chosen distance scales as the prior cost.

\subsection{The shared-data law and its affine heights}

Write $g_A^0$ and $g_A$ for the projected densities of $\sigma_d$ and $P$,
using the versions in \eqref{eq:finite-tilt}. Put
$\overline f(A,y)=g_A(y)/g_A^0(y)$ on the open ellipsoid. At labels with
$g_A(y)>0$, the posterior density on the corresponding uniform fiber is bounded by
$\exp(b_{A,y})$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-posterior-budget}
 b_{A,y}=b-\log\overline f(A,y)\ge0,\qquad
 \E b_{A,Y}=b-\E_A\KL(g_A\Vert g_A^0)\le b.
\end{equation}
These quantities are integrable: the positive density-ratio logarithm is
bounded by $b$, and its negative part is controlled by
$-z\log z\le1/e$ against the probability density $g_A^0$.

Given $(A,Y)$, draw $S_1,\ldots,S_q$ independently from the posterior.
Under this shared-data law, $AS_i=Y$, and each pair $(S_i,A)$ has the
original independent signal--matrix law. Define
\[
 F_i=\aff(S_1,\ldots,S_{i-1}),\qquad
 \rho_i=\dist(S_i,F_i),\qquad 2\le i\le q.
\]
The next estimate is stronger than merely proving logarithmic
integrability: its prior cost is divided by the ambient dimension.

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic cost of a height]\label{lem:distance-heights}
The heights are positive almost surely and obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-height}
 \rho_i\le2,\qquad
 \E\log^-\rho_i\le C\left(1+\frac bd\right),
 \qquad \log^-x:=\max\{0,-\log x\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We first record a uniform-sphere distance estimate. Let $U$ be uniform on
$S^{n-1}$ with $n\le d$, and let $E$ be a linear subspace of dimension
$j$ whose codimension $h=n-j$ is at least $d/2$. Write a normalized
Gaussian representation with independent components $X\in\R^h$ and
$Z\in\R^j$. For $t\ge\log2$,
\[
 \dist(U,E)<e^{-t}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \norm X\le2e^{-t}\norm Z.
\]
The Gaussian density bound and the unit-ball volume estimate give
$\Prob\{\norm X\le a\}\le(Ca/\sqrt h)^h$. Also
\[
 \E e^{\norm Z^2/4}=2^{j/2},\qquad
 \norm z^h\le(2h)^{h/2}e^{\norm z^2/4}.
\]
Since $j\le d\le2h$, these inequalities yield
$\E\norm Z^h\le(C\sqrt h)^h$. Hence
\[
 \Prob\{\dist(U,E)<e^{-t}\}\le(Ce^{-t})^h.
\]
If the law of $U$ is reweighted by a density at most $e^a$, the right side
is multiplied by $e^a$ and capped by one. Integrating the logarithmic tail
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-linear-log}
 \E\log^-\dist(U,E)\le C+\frac ah.
\end{equation}
The case $j=0$ is immediate and also satisfies this bound.

Condition now on $(A,Y,S_1,\ldots,S_{i-1})$. Let $r$ and $s_0$ be the
radius and center of the posterior fiber, and write
$u_j=(S_j-s_0)/r$. The vectors lie on the unit sphere in $\ker A$, of
ambient dimension $n=d-k$, and the next vector has density at most
$e^{b_{A,Y}}$ there. Moreover
\[
 \frac{\rho_i}{r}
 =\dist(u_i,\aff(u_1,\ldots,u_{i-1}))
 \ge\dist(u_i,\operatorname{span}(u_1,\ldots,u_{i-1})).
\]
The latter span has dimension at most $i-1$ and codimension at least
$d-k-q+1\ge d/2$. Apply \eqref{eq:distance-linear-log} and then
\eqref{eq:distance-posterior-budget}. The additional contribution
$\E[-\log r]$ is bounded by an absolute constant: for each fixed signal,
rotational invariance of the independent row space of $A$ makes $r$ a
uniform-vector distance to a $k$-dimensional linear space, so
\eqref{eq:distance-linear-log} applies with $a=0$.
This proves the negative-log bound. A zero height would make that
expectation infinite, so heights are positive almost surely. The bound
$\rho_i\le2$ follows because all signals are on the unit sphere.
\end{proof}

Set
\[
 h_k=\E[-\log g_A(Y)].
\]
This is finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:replica-logs}. Apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:replica-likelihood} with its row count equal to the
present $k$, its difference count equal to $q-1$, and its replica count
equal to $q$. The required relation
$k+q-1\le3d/16-1\le d-2$ holds in the present dimensions. The exact
likelihood is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-likelihood}
 \frac{dQ}{d\bigl(P^{\otimes q}(d\mathbf s)\gamma_{k,d}^{F}(dA)\bigr)}
 =(2\pi)^{-k(q-1)/2}J(\mathbf s)^{-k}g_A(As_1)^{1-q}.
\end{equation}
Here $Q$ is the shared-data law and $F$ is the span of all difference
columns. The evaluated density uses the exact versions established in
Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-label-measure}.

Since $J=\prod_{i=2}^q\rho_i$, all logarithms in
\eqref{eq:distance-likelihood} are integrable by
Lemma~\ref{lem:distance-heights} and Lemma~\ref{lem:replica-logs}.
Data processing after forgetting $A$ gives the finite upper bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-correlation-upper}
 \tc(S_1,\ldots,S_q)
 \le(q-1)h_k-k\sum_{i=2}^q\E\log\rho_i
             -\frac{k(q-1)}2\log(2\pi).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Projected height bins}

Draw the independent side matrix $B$ and put $C_i=BS_i$. Conditional on
the tuple, the image of the direction space of $F_i$ has rank $i-2$
almost surely. The orthogonal residual of $S_i$ from $F_i$ has length
$\rho_i$. Its image under $B$ is an $N(0,\rho_i^2 I_p)$ vector,
independent of the restriction of $B$ to that direction space.
Projecting off the image of the direction space shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-projected-height}
 T_i:=\dist(C_i,BF_i)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{law}}=\ \rho_i\chi_{m_i},
 \qquad m_i=p-i+2,
\end{equation}
conditional on the tuple. Here $\chi_{m_i}$ is a chi variable. In
particular $m_i\ge2k$.

Define the integer bin
\[
 K_i=\left\lceil\log_2(T_i/\sqrt{m_i})\right\rceil,
\]
with any fixed value at $T_i=0$, a null event. Use the reference
probabilities $\pi_j=2^{-|j|}/3$ for $j\in\mathbb Z$; they sum to one.
The chi-square second moment and the Gaussian small-ball bound show
uniformly in $m\ge1$ that
\[
 \E\log(\chi_m/\sqrt m)\le0,\qquad
 \E|\log(\chi_m/\sqrt m)|\le C.
\]
For the first inequality use Jensen on the square. For the second, its
positive part follows from the second moment, while the negative part follows
by integrating
$\Prob\{\chi_m/\sqrt m<e^{-t}\}\le\min\{1,(Ce^{-t})^m\}$.
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:distance-heights}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-bin-budget}
 (\log2)\E K_i\le\E\log\rho_i+\log2,\qquad
 H(K_i)\le\E[-\log\pi_{K_i}]
       \le C\left(1+\frac bd\right).
\end{equation}
The entropy bound is the elementary cross-entropy inequality, since
$-\log\pi_j=\log3+|j|\log2$ and the expected absolute bin index is bounded.

For fixed $B$, let $P_B$ be the law of $BS'$ for $S'\sim P$. Given
$(B,C_1,\ldots,C_{i-1})$, the affine set $BF_i$ is known. Let $E_j$ be its
tube of radius $\sqrt{m_i}\,2^j$. The bin event $K_i=j$ is contained in
$E_j$. Since the marginal of $C_i$ given $B$ is $P_B$, conditional relative
entropy followed by the bin map yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-bin-information}
 I(C_i;C_1,\ldots,C_{i-1}\mid B)
 \ge-H(K_i\mid B,C_1,\ldots,C_{i-1})-\E\log P_B(E_{K_i}).
\end{equation}
To justify the logarithms, coordinatewise data processing makes the
projected total correlation finite by
\eqref{eq:distance-correlation-upper}. Each conditional divergence in its
chain rule is therefore finite. Its image under the bin map is finite as
well. The expected negative logarithm of the reference bin probability is
that image divergence plus the finite conditional bin entropy. Since the
tube probability is at least the bin probability,
$-\log P_B(E_{K_i})$ is integrable. This also proves
\eqref{eq:distance-bin-information} without an undefined entropy difference.

\subsection{A fixed mixture before averaging the side matrix}

We will prove the one-height estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-tube-goal}
 \E\log P_B(E_{K_i})
 \le k\E\log\rho_i-h_k+C\left(k+1+\frac bd\right).
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{eq:distance-bin-information}, this recovers the
terms $h_k-k\E\log\rho_i$ in the unprojected correlation bound.
The selected bin $K_i$ depends on $B$. We first bound every fixed bin
using a reference density that depends only on $A$, so that the eventual
label average uses the original density $g_A$.

Fix the shared-data tuple and its $(A,Y)$, and fix $i$. For a candidate
$s'$, put $\rho'=\dist(s',F_i)$. Its distance to $BF_i$ after projection
has law $\rho'\chi_{m_i}$, by the same calculation as
\eqref{eq:distance-projected-height}. The Gaussian small-ball bound gives
\[
 \E_B P_B(E_j)
 \le\int\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{4\cdot2^j}{\rho'}\right)^{m_i}\right\}P(ds').
\]
The minimum is one at zero denominator. Since $A$ is constant with value
$Y$ on $F_i$,
$\norm{As'-Y}\le\norm A_{\op}\rho'$. Pushing $P$ forward by $A$ therefore
gives the further bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-tube-kernel}
 \E_B P_B(E_j)
 \le\int\min\left\{1,
       \left(\frac{4\cdot2^j\norm A_{\op}}{\norm{y'-Y}}\right)^{m_i}\right\}
       g_A(y')\,dy'.
\end{equation}

For $m>k$ and $\beta>0$, radial integration in $\R^k$ gives the exact
kernel mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-kernel-mass}
 \int_{\R^k}\min\{1,(\beta/\norm u)^m\}\,du
 =v_k\beta^k\frac{m}{m-k}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the inside ball contributes $v_k\beta^k$, and the exterior contributes
$kv_k\beta^k/(m-k)$. Let $a_{A,j}$ be the probability density obtained
by convolving $g_A$ with this normalized kernel, using
$m=m_i$ and $\beta=4\cdot2^j\norm A_{\op}$. Since $m_i\ge2k$ and
$v_k\le(2\pi e/k)^{k/2}$, \eqref{eq:distance-tube-kernel} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-fixed-bin}
 2^{-kj}\E_B P_B(E_j)
 \le\left(\frac{C\norm A_{\op}}{\sqrt k}\right)^k a_{A,j}(Y).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $i$, this density depends on $A$ and $j$, but not on the
replica tuple and not on $Y$ as a parameter of the density.

Define
\[
 a_A=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\pi_j a_{A,j},\qquad
 T_B=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\pi_j2^{-kj}P_B(E_j).
\]
The first is a probability density depending only on $A$ and the fixed
index $i$. Conditional on the shared-data outcome,
\eqref{eq:distance-fixed-bin} gives
\[
 \E_B T_B\le
 \left(\frac{C\norm A_{\op}}{\sqrt k}\right)^k a_A(Y).
\]
The $j=K_i$ summand gives the pointwise inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-mixture}
 \log P_B(E_{K_i})
 \le kK_i\log2-\log\pi_{K_i}
   +\log T_B.
\end{equation}

Before averaging logarithms, we verify their integrability. If
$Z_{A,j}=v_k(4\cdot2^j\norm A_{\op})^k m_i/(m_i-k)$, then for $y$ in
the support of $g_A$,
\[
 \frac{\pi_0}{Z_{A,0}}\le a_A(y)\le\sup g_A.
\]
The lower bound holds because the support has diameter at most
$2\norm A_{\op}$, so the unnormalized kernel at $j=0$ equals one throughout
that support. The upper bound follows by convolving a bounded density with
a probability density. The positive log of $\sup g_A$ is integrable by the
uniform projected-density formula and the Gaussian log-determinant bound.
The absolute log of $Z_{A,0}$ is integrable: $\log^+\norm A_{\op}$ is
controlled by its finite mean, and
$\log^-\norm A_{\op}\le\log^-\norm{Au_0}$ for any fixed unit $u_0$, whose
norm is chi-distributed. Thus $\log a_A(Y)$ is absolutely integrable.

The pointwise inequality
\eqref{eq:distance-mixture} bounds $\log^-T_B$ by the sum of
$-\log P_B(E_{K_i})$, $k|K_i|\log2$, and $-\log\pi_{K_i}$, all integrable.
For the positive part,
$\E_B\log^+T_B\le\log(1+\E_BT_B)$, and
\eqref{eq:distance-fixed-bin} bounds its right side using $a_A(Y)$ and
$\norm A_{\op}$ as above. Thus $\log T_B$ is absolutely integrable as well.

Conditional Jensen now gives
\[
 \E_B\log T_B\le
 k\log\left(\frac{C\norm A_{\op}}{\sqrt k}\right)+\log a_A(Y).
\]
The right side depends only on $(A,Y)$. In the full experiment
$Y\mid A$ has density $g_A$, and nonnegativity of
$\KL(g_A\Vert a_A)$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-reference-density}
 \E\log a_A(Y)\le\E\log g_A(Y)=-h_k.
\end{equation}
This is where the fact that $a_A$ depends only on $A$ is needed.
Also $\E\log(C\norm A_{\op}/\sqrt k)\le C$ by the Gaussian norm
estimate in Section~\ref{sec:gaussian}, since $k\ge d/32$. Consequently
$\E\log T_B\le Ck-h_k$. Combining this with
\eqref{eq:distance-mixture} and \eqref{eq:distance-bin-budget} proves
\eqref{eq:distance-tube-goal}.

Insert \eqref{eq:distance-tube-goal} in
\eqref{eq:distance-bin-information}, use the entropy bound
in \eqref{eq:distance-bin-budget}, and sum the conditional divergences
over $i=2,\ldots,q$. The projected correlation satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance-correlation-lower}
 \tc(C_1,\ldots,C_q\mid B)
 \ge(q-1)h_k-k\sum_{i=2}^q\E\log\rho_i
          -C(q-1)\left(k+1+\frac bd\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:distance-block}]
Generate $W$ from the shared data independently of the replicas conditional
on $(A,Y)$. Each replica has the original signal--message pair law, and
$B$ is independent of this coupling. The finite correlation bound
\eqref{eq:distance-correlation-upper} therefore permits
Lemma~\ref{lem:replica-information} with $t=q$:
\[
 qI(S;W\mid B,BS)
 \le H(W)+\tc(S_1,\ldots,S_q)-\tc(C_1,\ldots,C_q\mid B).
\]
Subtract \eqref{eq:distance-correlation-lower} from
\eqref{eq:distance-correlation-upper}. The measurement entropy and all
height logarithms cancel. Since $k\le d/16$, division by $q$ gives
\eqref{eq:distance-block}. Section~\ref{sec:streaming-consequences} applies
this bound while conditioning only on the current state and retaining its
separate late elimination of the clock cost.
\end{proof}
